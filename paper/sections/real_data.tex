\section{Empirical Illustration}\label{sec:realdata}

The empirical illustration uses NHANES data, with age decade as the subgroup variable. Complete cases for AgeDecade, BMI, Gender, Poverty, DirectChol, Pulse, and BPSysAve yield $n=7116$ observations in eight groups: ages 0 to 9 ($n=186$), 10 to 19 ($n=1080$), 20 to 29 ($n=1126$), 30 to 39 ($n=1138$), 40 to 49 ($n=1217$), 50 to 59 ($n=1116$), 60 to 69 ($n=782$), and 70 or older ($n=471$).

The pooled model is
\[
\mathrm{BMI} \sim \mathrm{Gender} + \mathrm{Poverty} + \mathrm{DirectChol} + \mathrm{Pulse} + \mathrm{BPSysAve},
\]
and the same formula is fitted separately within each age decade. We then compute sign stability, standardized drift, context-normalized drift, and predictor-level summaries.

The aggregate summary shows substantial variation across age groups: mean sign stability is $0.814$, mean standardized drift is $16.203$, and mean context-normalized drift is $11.339$.

Among the predictors, BPSysAve has the lowest sign stability in this specification. Its SSR is $0.571$, standardized drift is $32.070$, and context-normalized drift is $17.641$. Gendermale and DirectChol have SSR equal to one, although DirectChol still has appreciable magnitude drift.

Figure~\ref{fig:nhanes-bpsys-ci} shows the BPSysAve coefficient and its 95\% confidence interval in each age group, together with the pooled estimate. The estimated coefficient declines with age and changes sign.

The youngest group is relatively small ($n=186$), so we repeated the analysis after excluding ages 0 to 9. Several drift values decreased, but the BPSysAve result remained: drift fell from $32.070$ to $28.696$ and SSR changed from $0.571$ to $0.524$. The instability is therefore not attributable solely to the smallest group. This sensitivity check does not establish a distinct age-specific mechanism; nonlinear age effects and omitted variables remain possible explanations.

We compared the pooled and age-specific models within each age group. The age-specific model had lower RMSE and higher $R^2$ in every group. The largest difference occurred for ages 0 to 9, where pooled $R^2=-3.485$ and subgroup $R^2=0.241$. Improvements also remained in the oldest group, where pooled $R^2=-0.086$ and subgroup $R^2=0.130$.

We then conducted 20 repetitions of five-fold cross-validation, assigning folds separately within age groups. Across the 800 held-out group-fold evaluations, the average pooled RMSE exceeded the age-specific RMSE by $0.911$. The repeated-CV interval was $[0.813, 1.008]$; this interval describes variation across repeated fold assignments and is not treated as a formal population confidence interval. The pooled model had higher average held-out RMSE in every age group. The largest difference was for ages 0 to 9 ($4.461$), but positive differences remained in the older groups, including $0.613$ for ages 70 and older. Thus, the in-sample pattern is also present in repeated held-out predictions for this specification. Table~\ref{tab:nhanes-cv} reports the group-level estimates and their intervals.
